SECTION 1

Vision & Executive Summary

Bias Buster is an interactive judicial simulation game that trains players to make objective, merit-based decisions while actively resisting unconscious cognitive biases and superficial distractions.

Key Design Pillars

  • Experiential Learning: Rather than telling players about bias, the game actively lures players into cognitive traps (halo effect, social proof, affinity bias) and exposes their blindspots in real-time.
  • Dual-Layer Evaluation: Players evaluate both who to pick and why by categorizing individual profile data points into Merit vs. Distraction.
  • Empirical Feedback Loops: Instant post-round audits map decisions directly against quantified merit scores, providing actionable psychology-backed insight.
SECTION 2

Core Game Mechanics

1. Profile Evaluation Phase

In each round, players receive a judicial bench assignment containing 3 candidate dossiers. Profiles are designed with equal textual length but contrasting internal validity.

🟢 True Merit Signals

  • Verified quantitative accomplishments (e.g. "Increased server throughput by 42%")
  • Peer-reviewed certifications & patents
  • Demonstrated problem-solving metrics
  • Directly relevant tenure & domain expertise

🔴 Superficial Distractions

  • Attractive headshots & physical fashion choices
  • Social media follower counts & viral fame
  • Shared hobbies, alma maters, or personal affinity
  • Buzzword fluency without underlying metrics

2. Interactive Tagging Mechanic

Players do not just click a button to pick a winner. They can click on specific attribute badges on any candidate's profile to mark them as either Merit (Green) or Distraction (Purple). This measures their active signal-to-noise recognition ratio.

SECTION 3

Scoring System Architecture

The total score for each round is calculated using a multi-factor mathematical formula designed to reward consistency, accuracy, and rigorous reasoning.

Round Score = (Base Selection Score + Reasoning Bonus - Distraction Penalty) × Consistency Multiplier
Scoring Factor Point Value Condition / Rule
Correct Candidate Pick +100 Points Awarded when selecting candidate with highest mathematical merit score.
Suboptimal Candidate Pick +25 Points Selected candidate has moderate merit, but not the top performer.
Distraction Trap Pick 0 Points (-30 Penalty) Selected candidate was heavily skewed by superficial hype/beauty with poor merit.
Tagging Precision Bonus Up to +50 Points +10 pts per correctly classified attribute tag (Merit vs Noise).
Consistency Multiplier 1.0x to 2.5x Increases by +0.3x for each consecutive correct round. Resets on falling for bias.
SECTION 4

Round Structure & Progression Architecture

The game consists of 6 core rounds, each specifically engineered around a distinct cognitive bias psychological phenomenon:

R1

The Halo Effect (Visual & Aesthetics Bias)

Pits an ultra-glamorous, immaculately dressed candidate with mediocre stats against an unpolished candidate with ground-breaking research publications.

R2

Social Proof & Popularity Hype

Features a viral tech influencer candidate with 250k followers vs a quiet engineer responsible for critical open-source infrastructure.

R3

In-Group Affinity Bias

Tests whether the judge favors a candidate who shares their personal hometown, favorite band, and alma mater over a candidate with superior domain metrics.

R4

Prestige & Brand-Name Fallacy

Exposes reliance on elite university titles or big tech company logos vs real individual execution and problem-solving impact.

R5

Confidence & Charisma vs. Substance Trap

Features a candidate using high-powered corporate jargon and assertive delivery vs an introverted candidate with rigorous empirical testing proof.

R6

Final Boss: The Multi-Bias Crucible

Combines visual charm, social clout, prestige titles, and affinity markers into a masterclass profile evaluation trial.

SECTION 6

Feedback System & Cognitive Psychology

Effective bias mitigation requires actionable, psychological feedback loops. Every post-round feedback report contains three crucial components:

🎯

Direct Empirical Mirror

Shows side-by-side comparison of candidate true merit scores vs player choice score.

🧠

Psychological Naming

Explains the exact cognitive bias trap (e.g. Confirmation Bias, In-Group Favoritism) that triggered the misstep.

💡

Mitigation Heuristic

Provides a practical rule of thumb for future evaluations (e.g. "Blinded Auditing", "Metric Stripping").
